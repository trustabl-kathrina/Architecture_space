## Writing principles

1. **Research-first** — Every material factual claim (vendor capability, metric, protocol behavior, regulatory requirement, product limit) must trace to the research context. Cite inline as `[source_id]` immediately after the claim. If no source supports a claim, omit it or label it clearly as practitioner judgment.

2. **Section expertise** — Write from deep familiarity with **this section's domain** (not generic “IT architecture”). Use correct terminology, name the right standards and patterns (e.g. DAMA, TOGAF viewpoints, OpenTelemetry, mesh/fabric/lakehouse patterns) when the section calls for them. Explain *why* choices matter for enterprise scale, governance, cost, and risk.

3. **Enterprise lens** — Address multi-team ownership, platform boundaries, security and privacy, FinOps, operability, and evolution over a 3–5 year horizon. Include at least one **concrete enterprise scenario** (industry-agnostic is fine) that shows how architects apply this section in practice.

4. **Decision-ready** — Where the template allows, add decision triggers, trade-off tables, or “when to use / when to avoid” guidance so readers can act. Distinguish **context** (why it matters), **mechanism** (how it works), and **implications** (what to decide).

5. **Architecture Space style** — Clear Markdown. Short paragraphs. Meaningful `##` / `###` headings matching the section spec and template catalog. Use tables for comparisons, scope, or maturity snapshots. Use `mermaid` diagrams only when they clarify architecture flow or component relationships—not decoration.

6. **Precision over volume** — Meet the word target in the section spec without padding. Prefer dense, scannable prose over long narratives. Bullet lists for capabilities, risks, and checklists; prose for causality and trade-offs.

7. **Consistency** — Align naming with the section title, taxonomy ID, and sibling topics in the spec. Do not invent product names, benchmarks, or citations that are absent from research context.
