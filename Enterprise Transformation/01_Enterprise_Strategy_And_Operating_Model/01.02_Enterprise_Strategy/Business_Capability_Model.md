# Business Capability Model

## Definition
A Business Capability Model (BCM) represents "what" the business does, defining the organization's capacity to execute its business model.

## Core Capabilities (Level 1 & Level 2 Examples)

### 1.0 Customer Management
- 1.1 Customer Acquisition
- 1.2 Customer Relationship Management
- 1.3 Customer Analytics

### 2.0 Product Management
- 2.1 Product Development
- 2.2 Product Lifecycle Management
- 2.3 Pricing Strategy

### 3.0 Supply Chain & Logistics
- 3.1 Procurement
- 3.2 Inventory Management
- 3.3 Fulfillment & Delivery

### 4.0 Finance Management
- 4.1 Financial Planning & Analysis
- 4.2 Accounts Payable/Receivable
- 4.3 Tax & Treasury

## Architectural Usage
The BCM is used to:
- Map applications and data to specific business functions.
- Identify duplicate systems supporting the same capability (rationalization).
- Define boundaries for Domain-Driven Design and Data Mesh implementations.
